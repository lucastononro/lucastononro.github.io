I am **Lucas Tonon**. I work on machine learning — mostly post-training language
models and building the evaluation harnesses that tell you whether the
post-training did anything.

This is a notebook, not a publication. Things get written here when I have
worked something out well enough to explain it and not so well that it is
obvious. The bar is: *would this have saved me a day two months ago?* Posts are
short, they have equations where equations are the shortest way to say
something, and they are wrong occasionally — corrections are appended in place
rather than quietly edited away.

The recurring themes:

- **Post-training and RL.** Reward design as estimator design; what verifiable
  rewards do and do not buy you.
- **Measurement.** Benchmarks that measure what they claim to, and the much
  larger set that do not.
- **Training dynamics.** Optimizer behaviour, loss curves, and the arithmetic
  behind rules of thumb everyone repeats.
- **Representation.** Tokenizers, context, and how input encoding quietly
  determines what a model can express.

## The site

Static HTML generated by about four hundred lines of Node in
[this repository](https://github.com/lucastononro/ablations) — no framework, no
build step beyond `node build.mjs`. Markdown in, letterpress out.

Syntax highlighting is done at build time with Shiki, so there is no
highlighter shipped to your browser. Mathematics is rendered at build time with
KaTeX, so equations do not reflow after paint. Margin notes are pure CSS.
Total client-side JavaScript: a theme toggle and a keyboard handler for the
margin notes, about thirty lines.

Set in [Instrument Serif](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Instrument+Serif)
for display, [Newsreader](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Newsreader) for
text, and IBM Plex Mono for anything that is really a label.

There is [an RSS feed](/feed.xml). There is no newsletter, no
analytics, and no cookie banner, because there are no cookies.
